What this is

A hierarchical explorer of the Pali canon, built on the segment-aligned bilara-data corpus (Mahāsaṅgīti edition; translations by Bhikkhu Sujato and Bhikkhu Brahmali where available). Each level of the canon — sound, word, segment, discourse, collection, basket — gets its own feature signal and its own view. This first release ships the discourse level (the map) and the collection level (the tree).

How the map is made

Each of the 6,959 texts is represented by TF-IDF weights over a 40,000-term Pali vocabulary, reduced to 100 dimensions with truncated SVD, then laid out in 2D with UMAP on cosine distance. Points near each other share vocabulary. The colors are the traditional groupings — the layout knows nothing about them, which is what makes the agreements and the mixtures interesting.

How the tree is made

Collections (saṁyuttas, nipātas, books) are averaged into lexical centroids and clustered bottom-up. The result is a tree inferred purely from word use, displayed against the canonical hierarchy. Where the inferred tree keeps canonical siblings together, vocabulary tracks the traditional structure; where it doesn't, something more interesting is going on — genre cutting across baskets, late texts clustering by register, formulaic material finding its kin.

Caveats

Lexical similarity is not doctrine, chronology, or authorship. UMAP distorts global distances by design. Collection centroids blur internal diversity. Treat everything here as a lens for finding questions, not answers.
