What this is

A hierarchical explorer of the Pali canon, built on the segment-aligned bilara-data corpus (Mahāsaṅgīti edition; translations by Bhikkhu Sujato and Bhikkhu Brahmali where available). Each level of the canon — sound, word, segment, discourse, collection, basket — gets its own feature signal and its own view. The current release is a single four-panel instrument: a curated taxonomy browser, the concept map and the text map (both t-SNE over one shared SVD space, linked by a full-text term–document matrix), and a reader with per-word dictionary lookup (NCPED via sc-data). Collection-level tree inference lives in the analysis pipeline and will return in a future pass.

How the map is made

Each of the 6,959 texts is represented by TF-IDF weights over a 40,000-term Pali vocabulary, reduced to 100 dimensions with truncated SVD, then laid out in 2D with UMAP on cosine distance. Points near each other share vocabulary. The colors are the traditional groupings — the layout knows nothing about them, which is what makes the agreements and the mixtures interesting.

How the panels link

The term map and the text map are joined by a term–document incidence matrix over the full Pali text. Click a term to see every text that contains it; click a text to see its vocabulary footprint and read it, Pali and English interleaved; click any Pali word in the reader to look it up and locate it among the concepts.

Caveats

Lexical similarity is not doctrine, chronology, or authorship. UMAP distorts global distances by design. Collection centroids blur internal diversity. Treat everything here as a lens for finding questions, not answers.
